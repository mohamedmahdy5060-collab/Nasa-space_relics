Relic Registry

One public record for every machine NASA left on the Moon and Mars. Each entry tells the story, and the same data helps NASA protect assets, plan missions and cut costs. The registry also logs landers from other countries and companies, so it works as one ledger for everything humans have put on the surface.

1. The Registry

Story on the surface, usable data underneath.

Reuse Score is our own team assessment (ease of reaching it, recoverable material, science value), not NASA data. Facts come from the NASA pages in the museum's source archive.

2. Landing Verification

For lander companies and insurers: independent proof of where a lander ended up.

This has happened for real. In 2019 India's Vikram lander lost contact just before touchdown. An Indian engineer compared old and new images from NASA's Moon orbiter and spotted debris about 750 m from the main crash site, and NASA confirmed it. A registry can turn that into a repeatable service.

Enter two points and press Build report.

Concept: compare the planned point with where images show the lander. A real report would attach the before and after image pair.

3. Change Detector

Compares an old and a new image of the same spot and flags what is different. It supports verification, the volunteer tasks and the material study.

Illustration only, not a real image. A real version loads NASA before and after image pairs.
